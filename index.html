! Recovery gear can kill

A strap, rope, shackle or recovery point that lets go under load flies with enough energy to kill or maim. The warning the Australian standard puts on every strap says it plainly: occupants and bystanders have been killed by flying fittings. This page is for information only. It works from published figures and a simple physics model; it can't see your recovery: the ground, the state of your gear, your recovery points, the anchor or who is standing where.

Your judgement on the scene comes first. A green tick here is not a go-ahead. If anything looks wrong, stop. Use rated gear on rated recovery points, fit safety lanyards at both ends, keep everyone well clear, and get proper training: the Australian strap standard tells users to consider a recognised four-wheel drive course (source).

    Disclaimer

    For information only. Not advice, not a guarantee, not a go-ahead. The peak force comes from a simple physics model, and straps, ropes, shackles and recovery points vary with make, age, wear, wet and damage. Every figure is linked to its source, but a real snatch can load gear harder than this page shows.

    Kinetic recovery is dangerous. The person running the recovery has to judge it on the scene, and that judgement always overrides this page. Use rated recovery points and rated gear, inspect it before every use, keep everyone well clear, fit safety lanyards at both ends, follow the instructions that came with your gear, and get trained.
